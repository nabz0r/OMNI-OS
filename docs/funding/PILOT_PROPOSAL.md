# OMNI four-week pilot proposal

Proposed commercial scope. This document does not form a signed agreement. Parties, tax treatment, liability, confidentiality and data-processing terms must be settled in the actual contract.

## Purpose and scope

Evaluate whether OMNI helps a fintech engineering or operations team reuse approved context across supported AI providers while understanding each disclosure. Limit the pilot to one recurring documentation workflow, up to ten consenting adult participants, and explicitly approved model accounts. Begin with synthetic examples and move only to public or approved non-sensitive material after the customer reviews the deployment.

The pilot excludes production financial decisions, customer identity files, payment credentials, personal financial data and customer-facing automated advice. It does not assess or promise system-wide DLP, protection from a compromised administrator, complete provider deletion or regulatory certification.

## Proposed offer

EUR 2,000 excluding tax for four weeks, including setup assistance, one workflow/policy configuration, weekly review and a final evidence report. Proposed payment is invoiced at completion with 30-day terms, consistent with the model's one-month collection lag. The customer supplies compatible hardware, model accounts, inference charges and an operational sponsor. Any additional integrations, users or on-site work require an agreed change of scope. These terms test willingness to pay; they are not an existing contract.

## Work plan

| Week | Activity | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Agree permitted data, baseline tasks and participants; install evaluation build and review boundaries | Signed scope, baseline timing, platform version and permissions record |
| 2 | Use selected memories on the same workflow; review errors and false policy blocks | Paired task observations and correction log |
| 3 | Repeat tasks, test revocation, provider changes and approved recovery scenarios | Receipt-to-permission review and incident register |
| 4 | Compare with the manual context brief; assess buying decision and support cost | Final report and explicit paid continuation decision |

Before any recovery claim, execute a supported backup/restore drill on synthetic data. If complete recovery remains unavailable, retain synthetic evaluation scope and do not rely on the app as the sole copy of work. A new security or privacy concern pauses the affected trial path immediately.

## Measurements and decision criteria

Record participants recruited, activated and completing four weeks separately. A useful session means completing the agreed task through a supported OMNI path with an applicable grant. Count activity using separately consented pilot records; the optional analytics collector is not a unique-person counter.

For each paired task, record task type, model/version, preparation time, completion result, incorrect/stale memory inclusion, policy false positives, support minutes and revocation outcome. Alternate order between OMNI and a manually maintained brief. Keep the same model and budget. Seek at least 30 paired tasks per pilot; do not present three such pilots as the larger 200-task utility study.

Proposed decision thresholds: all observed disclosures match permission, no unresolved high/critical bypass, at least 70% of activated participants complete a useful session in week four, and a documented buyer decision on a paid continuation. Evaluate the 30% context-time target descriptively at pilot scale, with sample sizes and uncertainty. A serious disclosure failure overrides favorable averages.

## Data handling and deliverables

The customer approves the material and providers. Each participant controls the selected local memories within the agreed workflow. Authorized content reaches the chosen provider. OMNI's operational metadata remains local unless deliberately exported. Export only the minimum aggregate study data, with explicit consent and a defined retention period in the signed scope. Do not treat telemetry opt-in as consent for all processing.

Deliver a setup record, tested policy examples, aggregate pilot report, open-issues list and a deletion/exit checklist. The exit checklist covers local memories, exported metadata and customer-controlled provider retention. Revocation cannot recover content already delivered to a provider.

## Discovery questions for the sponsor

1. Describe the last task where you rebuilt context for a different assistant. How often does that happen?
2. Show the current context brief or process using non-sensitive material. What is difficult about maintaining it?
3. Which data may be sent to which model accounts? Who approves that policy?
4. Who owns the budget, and what other tool or manual process would this replace?
5. What result would make you pay for a four-week evaluation? What would make you reject it?
6. What installation, recovery, procurement or support requirement would prevent a pilot?
7. If the pilot succeeds, who can approve a subscription and on what timetable?

Record actual answers, refusals and evidence. Do not turn an encouraging interview into a letter of intent.
